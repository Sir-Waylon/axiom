# How these trainers help you learn

Every design choice maps to learning-science findings. (Citations are from memory of well-known papers; please verify before quoting them.)

| Idea | What the app does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice beats rereading | Every card is followed by a question right away | Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013 |
| Spacing | Missed or shaky items return after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days (Leitner boxes) | Cepeda et al. 2006 |
| Interleaving | Practice mixes topics; same topic is not repeated back-to-back | Rohrer & Taylor 2007 |
| Desirable difficulty at ~85% success | The coach targets about 85% correct and raises difficulty after streaks | Wilson et al. 2019; Bjork 1994 |
| Worked examples and fading | A wrong answer opens a step-by-step explanation, then a similar problem | Sweller; Renkl & Atkinson |
| Hypercorrection | High-confidence errors are explained and revisited | Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001 |
| Feedback that is specific | Explains the reason, not just right/wrong | Hattie & Timperley 2007 |
| Self-determination (autonomy, competence, relatedness) | Choice of mode, test-out to skip, visible mastery, friend codes | Ryan & Deci 2000 |
| Goal gradient | Progress bars, weekly study-day goal | Kivetz et al. 2006 |
| Growth mindset praise | Praises effort and strategy; "Not yet" language | Mueller & Dweck 1998 |
| Active exploration | Sliders and mini-labs before problems | Brilliant-style learning by doing |
| Breaks and sleep | 3-minute brain break after 20 minutes; late-night and long-session nudges | Distributed practice research |

## What we deliberately avoid
No loot boxes, no real-money purchases, no coins that give advantages, no guilt streak-loss messages, no endless-scroll traps. Coins buy cosmetics only.

## Honest limits
The coach is a heuristic (Elo-style ability + Leitner review), simulated across ability levels; it has not been tested in a classroom. Treat it as a strong starting point and check student results.
