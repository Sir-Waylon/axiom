# Research behind the learning design

Each design choice below is tied to published work. These are summaries of well-known findings, not claims that this app has been shown to raise scores; no outcome study has been run.

| Feature | Idea | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Quizzes, flashcards, Blitz, Match | Retrieving an answer strengthens memory more than rereading | Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological Science* 17(3); Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1) |
| Daily mission mixes earlier units back in | Spreading practice over time improves retention | Cepeda et al. (2006), *Psychological Bulletin* 132(3) |
| Random question order, mixed topics | Interleaving different problem types improves later discrimination and transfer | Rohrer & Taylor (2007), *European Journal of Cognitive Psychology* 19(4-5) |
| Worked solutions and step-by-step explanations | Studying worked examples reduces load for novices | Sweller & Cooper (1985), *Cognition and Instruction* 2(1) |
| Easier "bridge" questions before hard ones | Fading support as skill grows; productive difficulty | Bjork & Bjork (2011), *Psychology and the Real World*; Kalyuga et al. (2003), *Educational Psychologist* 38(1) |
| Immediate feedback with an explanation | Feedback that says why helps most | Hattie & Timperley (2007), *Review of Educational Research* 77(1) |
| Diagnostic, then plan | Formative assessment that changes instruction raises achievement | Black & Wiliam (1998), *Assessment in Education* 5(1) |
| Short 15-minute missions, progress bar | Short, bounded tasks with visible progress support attention; progress feedback supports motivation | Amabile & Kramer (2011), *The Progress Principle* (practitioner source); Deci & Ryan (2000), *Psychological Inquiry* 11(4) |
| Animated, interactive demos and labs | Learners do better with words plus matched graphics, and with active manipulation | Mayer (2009), *Multimedia Learning*, 2nd ed.; Chi & Wylie (2014), *Educational Psychologist* 49(4) |
| Confidence chips ("Guess / Think so / Sure") | Calibrating confidence improves study decisions | Metcalfe (2009), *Current Directions in Psychological Science* 18(3) |
| Sleep-first coin bonus, after-school hours | Sleep supports memory consolidation; late-night study trades against sleep | Stickgold (2005), *Nature* 437; Paruthi et al. (2016), *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine* 12(6) |
| Cosmetic-only rewards, visible odds, no pay-to-win | Rewards that crowd out interest can hurt intrinsic motivation; rewards here never change questions or grades | Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999), *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6) |

## Limits
- Several findings come from adult or high-school samples; effects for 8th graders are expected to be similar but not guaranteed.
- Please check citations before quoting them. They were written from memory of the literature without opening each paper.
